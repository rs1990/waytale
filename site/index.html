The problem

You drove past something remarkable and never knew.

The information exists. It is simply never available at the moment you are actually standing in front of the thing.

Research does not survive the trip

Nobody reads three articles about a landmark while driving toward it. The context you wanted arrives days later, at home, when the moment has passed.

Guided tours are inflexible

A scheduled tour means a fixed route, a fixed time, and a group. Most travel does not work that way, and the roadside landmarks between destinations are never covered at all.

You cannot tell fact from folklore

Travel content mixes documented history with local legend and never says which is which. A good story and a true one are not the same thing, and you deserve to know the difference.

What you get

Written to be trusted, not just enjoyed.

The difference between WayTale and a generated travel blurb is what happens before you hear a single word.

Nothing plays unreviewed

Every story is written, checked against its sources, and approved by a person before it can reach a traveller. There is no path from raw draft to your speakers.

Fact and legend are labelled

Documented history, local folklore, and a mix of the two each carry their own tag, shown clearly while you listen. The story stays good and you still know what is true.

Sources you can follow

Every fact links back to the reference it came from, so a claim that surprises you can be checked in one tap rather than taken on faith.

Stories stay current

When a source is updated, the story is rewritten and reviewed again. The version you hear stays live until the replacement has been checked.

Routes worth taking

Plan a whole trip

Questions

Good questions to ask.

Is it safe to use while driving?

It is designed for it. Narration starts on its own when you come into range, so there is nothing to tap, read, or search while moving. The screen is there for planning before you set off and for reading more once you have stopped.

How do you know the stories are accurate?

Stories are written only from collected reference material rather than from open-ended generation, then checked by a person against those same sources before publication. Nothing reaches a traveller without that approval step.

What if a story is legend rather than history?

It is labelled as such. Content is tagged as verified, legend, or a mix of the two, and the tag is shown while you listen. Local stories are worth telling, and they are told as stories.

What is covered today?

All 63 US National Parks. More regions are being added, and the same review process applies to every one of them.

Does it work without a signal?

Not yet. The current version needs a connection to fetch nearby landmarks and their audio. Downloading an itinerary for offline use is planned and is not in the current build.

What stage is the product at?

Early access. The app, the story pipeline, and the review workflow all work end to end and the national parks are covered. Offline downloads, personalisation by interest, and additional languages are on the roadmap rather than available now.
